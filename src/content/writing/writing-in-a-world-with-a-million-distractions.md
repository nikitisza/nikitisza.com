<em>Why don’t people publish their writings online?</em>

<em>Why don’t people make those videos they always dream about making?</em>

<em>Why don’t people share their art online?</em>

The answer is <strong><em>they are afraid of what others think.</em></strong>

What their friends and family might think and what their colleagues might think.

If it’s you, you should stop worrying.

<strong>I get it.</strong>

One time, it was me too. Until I finally realized most people just simply don’t care. I believe:

<strong><em>People wouldn’t worry about what others think if they knew how hard it is to get attention online.</em></strong>

When we don’t post that article or share that video, we have the classic case of the <strong>*Spotlight effect*</strong>. This is the psychological phenomenon when people tend to believe they are being noticed more than they really are.

We think others think about us a lot, but the truth is <strong>*they don’t*</strong>. They don’t care about us as much as we believe they do.

I know it sounds rough.

<strong>But it’s a good thing for aspiring writers like me.</strong> You landed on this article, so I assume it’s also a good thing for you, too.

# Everyone’s lost in their own world

<strong>*Keep pouring your heart and soul*</strong> into your art, writing, or videos because people’s attention span is just getting shorter and shorter.

It’s super hard to get someone to read a 6-minute-long article or watch a video for longer than 2 minutes.

I don’t know why it happened.

I can only assume the <strong><em>‘tiktokification’</em></strong> of content and <strong><em>‘tinderification’</em></strong> of people might have something to do with it.

When dating apps make it easy for people to ditch other humans in 2 seconds…it was only a matter of time before this behaviour swooped into our everyday lives.

<strong><em>What’s the result?</em></strong>

<em>People learned to ditch content, too!</em>

Imagine your reader views your story on Medium, but a notification pops up from Instagram…<strong>*and just like that*</strong>, they are onto another new, shiny thing.

<em>Readers are not here for a long time.</em>

Medium awards writers who can keep readers’ attention span <strong>*longer than 30 seconds*</strong>. Crazy, right? That’s how easily people lose interest in content.

# Here’s what I can do (and what you can too)

## #1. I can keep readers hooked

<strong><em>How?</em></strong>

I can let out everything. No filters.

I can dive deeper into my thoughts and feelings to squeeze out those last bits from my heart and soul.

I can give my readers a funny story that entertains them, or a new view and angle on a topic.

I can edit the cr\*p out of my writing to make the article concise and to the point.

## #2. I can just keep writing

<strong><em>Because no one cares.</em></strong>

I’m new to this writing. Knowing the fact people have the attention span of a goldfish is <strong>*comforting*</strong>.

I can practise my writing in peace. I can practise in public and improve, <strong>*one story at a time*</strong>.

If you’re still with me, hats off to you! It means you’re not a goldfish, and I thank you for that.

I leave you with a question: <strong>as writers & content creators, what’s your move?</strong>
