In the past, I often participated in Inktober when I was more focused on my art.

Then I thought, why don’t I just do <strong><em>‘Writober’</em></strong> this year?

I want to publish more often, and the only way to do so is by <strong>*doing the work*</strong>.

My goal is to publish over <em>1,000 articles in 3 years.</em>

Despite having [self-published a book last year,](https://medium.com/the-side-hustle-club/a-year-of-selling-ebooks-on-gumroad-heres-what-doesn-t-work-f635c3f95952) I still have imposter syndrome. It’s strange, but for some reason, I can only believe I’m a **real writer** when I have a track record of 1,000 published articles.

Here’s my system for publishing every day in October on Medium.

# Prepare the Night Before

To set myself up for success, I prepare the night before.

You might’ve heard from other <strong>*morning writers*</strong> that they never start with a blank page.

<em>Neither do I.</em>

The night before I write, I prepare an outline. My outline is a bullet-pointed list of all the things I want to include in the article so that the following day, when I hit the keyboard, I have all the information I need.

Inspiration strikes me at odd hours, so sometimes the outline is an analog version on a sticky note.

# Have Multiple Outlines Ready

While I decide the night before to write about [My dog has more followers on Instagram than I do](https://medium.com/bouncin-and-behavin-blogs/my-dog-has-more-followers-on-instagram-than-i-do-60cd2d1a407a), sometimes I don’t feel like writing about it when I wake up.

Whenever that happens, I go to <strong>*my trusted spreadsheet*</strong>, where all my articles are tracked at every stage, and I pick another topic.

Usually, I have 3 articles in the <strong><em>‘Outline’ stage</em></strong>.

Whenever I am not in the mood to write about a certain topic, I move on to another <strong>*without forcing myself to put together that story*</strong>.

In my case, 3 outlines work well, but you may need only 2, or 5.

<strong>This method always works!</strong>

# Use ChatGPT for Research

Once I have the topic figured out, and if I feel the <strong><em>bullet-pointed list is too flat,</em></strong> I ask ChatGPT to suggest other ideas.

Here’s the prompt I first saw in one of [Corrie’s](https://medium.com/@corriewhowrites) articles. This is what it looks like:

<em>What are some unique perspectives or ‘hot takes’ or ‘contrary angles’ I could take for an article about \[insert your topic or article’s title\]? Give me a list.</em>

<strong><em>It always works like a charm.</em></strong>

I’ve always been able to use some points from the ChatGPT’s output. But if nothing else, the AI output makes my brain think about other angles to incorporate into my articles.

# Value & Word Count

Every day, I strive to publish **something of value**, not just something.

I believe in short-form content, and I can certainly deliver valuable content in a few hundred words, but I decided to keep Writober articles a bit ‘meatier’ whenever I can.

This means I keep my articles around 600 words or write until I surely provide my readers with value.

# Early Days, but So Far, So Good

<strong>This month, there is only one goal:</strong> submitting an article to a publication <strong>*every day*</strong> in October.

So far, I haven’t missed a day of submission; however, some larger publications might take a while to schedule my articles.
