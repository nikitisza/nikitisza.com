AI will not replace you as a designer, but a designer who’s using AI will replace you.

There’s so much talk about [Chat GPT](https://openai.com/blog/chatgpt/), [Dall-e](https://openai.com/dall-e-2/), and other AI-assisted tools. So I was curious and tested ChatGPT for a week to see if it was **really** that good.

In this article, I will show you how we designers can use the latest hottest AI tool, ChatGPT.

# How can chatGPT help designers?

Let’s ask chatGPT itself.

<figure><div><img loading="lazy" alt="" src="/images/writing/12-areas-chatgpt-helps-my-work-as-a-ux-designer/63bc184bb4f24642a29a902e_0-jslf9cqmbjxh9g1k.png"></div><figcaption>Screenshot of ChatGPT’s response to ‘how it can help designers’</figcaption></figure>

It seems I wasn’t specific enough with my question. So let’s ask ChatGPT again, now focusing on UX designers.

<figure><div><img loading="lazy" alt="" src="/images/writing/12-areas-chatgpt-helps-my-work-as-a-ux-designer/63bc184b7b1d5d30e015f926_0-bl7giiubruszzgvr.png"></div><figcaption>Screenshot of ChatGPT’s response to ‘how it can help UX designers’</figcaption></figure>

According to ChatGPT, it can help assist user experience (UX) designers with the followings:

-   *Generating text for use in UX design projects*, such as website copy, app descriptions, or onboarding screens
-   *Generating a list of ideas and suggestions for the onboarding flow*, such as introducing the app’s core features and functionality, highlighting the benefits of using the app, and providing tips and best practices for using the app effectively
-   ChatGPT could also be used to *generate* *ideas* *for user flows or wireframes* by providing suggestions for how a user might navigate through different screens or complete tasks within the app
-   It can help *generate ideas for UX design concepts*
-   It can help *provide inspiration for UX work*

For example, a UX designer could input a prompt into ChatGPT, such as

<em>“Design the onboarding flow for a new task management app”</em> and ChatGPT could generate a list of ideas and suggestions for the onboarding flow, such as introducing the app’s core features and functionality, highlighting the benefits of using the app, and providing tips and best practices for using the app effectively.

# What’s next?

## For everyone

As ChatGPT doesn’t make everyone a great writer overnight, Dall-e doesn’t make everyone a great artist overnight.

Even though there are Grammarly, Wordtune, Quillbot, and many other AI writing tools nowadays, they still haven’t replaced human editors.

It’s similar to Canva or Adobe Express; these tools haven’t replaced graphic designers either.

Sure, some business owners would rather use cheaper labour, but that’s ok.

There’ll always be a market for human-made products. It’s kind of like a hand-made item. It’s a premium.

Would you pay a premium for a handmade knitted cardigan?

I know, I would.

## For designers

I predict, soon enough, AI will be able to draw simple user flows and design screens.

You will enter a prompt, such as “<strong><em>design a login flow happy path for a pet sitter iOS application”</em></strong><em>.</em> Then ChatGPT or Dall-e will just do exactly that.

ChatGPT is something I never knew I needed.

But let’s always remember its constraints when we use ChatGPT to assist our work.

It even tells us about its limitations:

<strong><em>“It’s important to note that ChatGPT is a tool, and its output should be used as a starting point for further refinement and editing, rather than being used directly in a final design.”</em></strong> <em>— ChatGPT</em>

Never take its output at face value because ChatGPT is still learning.

It still produces incorrect answers, especially when you replace your Google search with ChatGPT prompts.

But instead, take its output as a starting point.

If you haven’t tried it yet, go ahead.

Let me know if you find another use case for it. I’d love to hear about your discovery.
