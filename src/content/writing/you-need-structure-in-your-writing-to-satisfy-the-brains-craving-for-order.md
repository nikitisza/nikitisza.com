The human brain loves order.

It likes patterns, consistency, and predictability.

When we encounter new information, our minds search for structure, <strong><em>allowing us to quickly process, retain, and relate to the content.</em></strong>

#### Writing without structure feels chaotic, leaving readers lost and overwhelmed.

# Ways to Add Structure to Your Writing

Here are the most popularly used structures by writers.

## **1 — Categorical or topical**

For example: breaking down a broader topic into categories or subtopics, like <em>categorizing wildlife into mammals, birds, reptiles, etc.</em>

## **2 — Cause & effect**

For example: discussing an event and its consequences like <em>‘Because I forgot my umbrella in my minivan, I ended up drenched in the rain.’</em>

## **3 — Chronological order**

For example: detailing events as they happen in time, such as <em>describing your day from when you wake up to go to bed.</em>

## **4 — Compare & contrast**

For example: highlighting similarities and differences. Think about *comparing apples to oranges* — they’re both fruits but taste so different, right?

## **5 — Order of importance**

For example: starting from the most to the least important points, or vice versa, like listing out your tasks, from <em>‘feeding my puppy’ (highest priority) to ‘organizing my bookshelf by colour’ (maybe later).</em>

## **6 — Problem & solution**

For example: identifying a problem and then proposing a solution, such as <em>‘Oh, no, my puppy accidentally peed on the carpet!’</em> and then <em>‘Let’s clean it up with paper towel and odor removal spray!’</em>

## **7 — Sequential or process**

For example: a step-by-step guide such as <em>assembly instructions for an IKEA furniture or a vegan pumpkin pie recipe.</em>

## **8 — Spatial or descriptive**

For example: organizing your words by location or appearance, like <em>describing a house starting from the entrance all the way up to the attic.</em>

# Key Takeaways

<strong><em>Decide which structure is best suited for a particular piece of content.</em></strong> For instance, in this article, I used alphabetical order (which is another way to add structure) to add order to the bulletpointed list above.

You can also blend multiple structures within a single article.

#### By structuring our articles, we give our readers a map that guides them from the opening to the last line, ensuring that our message is understood easily.
