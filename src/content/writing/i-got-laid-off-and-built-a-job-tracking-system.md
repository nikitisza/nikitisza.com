Last month, [I got laid off](https://www.nikitisza.com/writing/i-got-laid-off-from-my-ux-tech-job-and-heres-why-i-dont-see-myself-as-a-failure) from my well-paying UX tech job.

If you have ever looked for a job, you know that the job search process is not only hard but also emotionally challenging.

Now, I can’t make the process less emotional, but I could help it stay organized, which would make all the difference. 

I’ve created​​ a spreadsheet that can transform your job hunt from chaos to clarity, [download your copy](https://nikitisza.gumroad.com/l/jobapptracker) today (it’s free)!

## <strong>What’s inside?</strong>

In the spreadsheet, you can track your job applications because I know we all apply for <em>not 1 one but 50 jobs, especially in the current job market.</em>

Here's what’s included:

-   <strong>Application details:</strong> Note the job title, company name and when you applied, link to job board if applicable
-   <strong>Job description link:</strong> Save the job ad details to have it handy if/when you’re selected for an interview because jobs can disappear quickly
-   <strong>Key requirements:</strong> Record the salary range and experience level the company is seeking, also if you add a cover letter and what you said in it
-   <strong>Application status tracking:</strong> Which version of your resume did you send – as you might want to have multiple versions of your resume? What stage are you at? Are you at the screening call, technical interview, or final round? 
-   <strong>Interview notes:</strong> Note who you spoke with and any important points that came up

## **Why this system works**

I made this spreadsheet (because I love spreadsheets, lol) but also because I wanted to keep everything in one place so that I didn’t need to dig through emails to find application details. 

I also wanted to easily see which companies I might need to follow up with and learn from what's working (and what isn't) in my search, e.g. resume versions.

I also wanted to track my applications systematically to see patterns in how companies are responding to my resumes and to use these insights to refine my approach & revise resumes and portfolio pieces.

## **Make it your own**

My system provides a starting framework; adapt it to what works for you. Since I’ve been targeting product design roles, I added a column for portfolio review and another for take-home exercises. However, you might want to remove those columns if they are irrelevant to your job search. 

I wish you the best of luck in landing your next role!
