In software development, we have a [retrospective](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsoftwarequality/definition/Agile-retrospective#:~:text=An%20Agile%20retrospective%20is%20a,actions%20for%20improvement%20going%20forward.) session after each sprint.

The year in review is similar to a retro. So it makes perfect sense to me to look back at the past year and plan for the next one.

**2022**: what a year it has been.

# What I usually do during an end-of-year review

**Reflect on accomplishments**

Review what I achieved over the past year and celebrate successes.

**Identify areas of growth**

I note which areas need improvement or have room for development and set goals to work towards them in the coming year. I identify areas I care about and want to develop.

**Look ahead**

I create a plan for the upcoming year that takes into account any changes or improvements identified in the review process. I set short-term and long-term goals that can help keep me on track to reach my objectives.

Because I work in tech, I personally like to set [SMART goals](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/productivity/how-to-write-smart-goals#:~:text=What%20are%20SMART%20goals%3F,within%20a%20certain%20time%20frame.) when possible, even when it’s about my personal life.

I wrote a more detailed [article](https://nikitisza.medium.com/how-to-set-and-achieve-goals-new-year-new-goals-2019-f69831834717) about this a while back.

# Year compass

The method outlined above was exactly my way of reviewing my years before finding [Year Compass](https://yearcompass.com/).

I found Year Compass last year and waited for nearly a whole year to try it out.

Studies [like this](https://www.inc.com/jeff-haden/a-study-of-800-million-activities-predicts-most-new-years-resolutions-will-be-abandoned-on-january-19-how-you-cancreate-new-habits-that-actually-stick.html) show that most people abandon their new year’s resolutions by the **19th of January**.

Yes. That’s true. It’s not even 3 weeks!

## Ideal self

Why is it so hard to stick to a new year’s resolution? I possibly don’t have the right answer, but it might have something to do with us setting these goals thinking of what our *ideal self* would do.

And…let’s be honest here. How many of us behave how our *ideal self* would behave all the time?

I know I don’t.

I liked the idea of having a plan for an ideal self and a less ideal self mentioned in [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5C1clVOG5X4), so I came up with my version for 2023.

# Setting up goals with a system

<strong>I ask 2 questions:</strong>

1.  What would my ideal self create or do?
2.  What would my less ideal self create or do?

# Medium-ideal self

That’s when my medium-ideal self comes in.

My medium-ideal self won’t be ideal, but she also won’t be my worst self.

<strong>I’ll explain it through an example.</strong>

What it’ll mean for YouTube:

-   Ideal self creates 2 videos per week (1 long form, 1 short form)
-   Less ideal self (worst self) creates 1 video a month (1 short)
-   Medium-ideal self creates more than 1 video a month (1 long form, 1 short form at least) but fewer than 8 videos a month

What my medium ideal self can achieve will be somewhere between my ideal and less ideal me:
